# Evaluation — rule-v1 vs model-v1 vs model-v2 vs model-v3 (synthetic data)

All traffic is synthetic, generated by model/gen_data.py. Numbers are
reported as computed, including where a model loses. rule-v1 is the
fixed rule deployed at M2: destination in history/known list -> LOW;
amount >= 0.008 ETH -> HIGH; first-seen destination -> MEDIUM; else LOW.
One rule, unchanged, for every vault. The deployed profile is
daily-small; monthly-large appears in the two-personality section.

## Held-out normal traffic (counts of verdicts)

Set A uses the v1 generator (no large-to-known pattern); set B uses the
v2 generator, whose normal traffic includes rare large payments to
known destinations. A HIGH on normal traffic freezes all three vaults
until the recovery key intervenes; a MEDIUM vetoes one withdrawal.
Under model-v1, every HIGH in set A was a 2.2x-4.3x-median payment to
a KNOWN address — the pattern generator v2 adds to normal data.

### Set A (v1 generator), daily-small, n=236

| method | LOW | MEDIUM | HIGH | flagged >=MEDIUM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rule-v1 | 234 | 0 | 2 | 2 of 236 |

## Attacks on daily-small (withdrawal # at first flag: >=MEDIUM / HIGH)

| scenario | rule-v1 | model-v1 | model-v2 | model-v3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full drain: 0.475 ETH to fresh addr | 1 / 1 | 1 / 1 | 1 / 1 | 1 / 1 |
| barrage: 10 x 0.004 ETH to fresh addr, 5 min apart | 1 / - | 1 / 1 | 1 / 1 | 1 / 7 |
| barrage, zero interval: 10 x 0.004 ETH to fresh addr, same block | 1 / - | 1 / 1 | 1 / 1 | 1 / 7 |
| concentration: 5 x 0.015 ETH to one fresh addr, 1 h apart | 1 / 1 | 1 / - | 1 / - | 2 / - |

Note: in the barrages, model-v1/v2 score the first withdrawal HIGH
already — the driver is the first-seen + small-amount combination
(rare in training), not pace accumulation. model-v3 flags >=MEDIUM
at #1 and reaches HIGH at #7 as its pace features accumulate. The
zero-interval barrage is additionally capped on-chain: the vault's
instant cap routes same-block bursts into the waiting queue, where
a veto can still reach them (see the GuardedVault tests).

## The structural scenarios: primer, then drain to a 'known' address

rule-v1's first branch trusts any destination that appears in the
request history. Two ways to exploit that, and what each method does:

**(a) The primer itself** — one tiny 0.001 ETH request to a fresh address:

| probe | rule-v1 | model-v1 | model-v2 | model-v3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| verdict on the primer | MEDIUM | HIGH | HIGH | MEDIUM |

model-v1/v2 call the primer itself HIGH (a tiny amount to a first-seen
address is a rare combination in training), so on-chain the vaults
would freeze at the primer; model-v3 and rule-v1 veto it as MEDIUM.
Either way the primer does not pass silently — but a defender cannot
rely on the attacker needing one:

**(b) Slow drain to an address already in the history** — e.g. a payee
whose receiving address the attacker controls. No primer, no new
destination, every amount below rule-v1's HIGH threshold:

| scenario | rule-v1 | model-v1 | model-v2 | model-v3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| slow drain: 20 x 0.0079 ETH to an address already in history, 10 min apart (first flag >=MED / HIGH) | - / - | - / - | - / - | 2 / - |

Under the 6-feature models (v1/v2) this drain is a genuine blind spot:
the 1-hour pace features plateau as the window slides, and every step
scores LOW. model-v3 adds 24-hour pace aggregates (f7/f8) aimed exactly
here. What bounds the damage regardless of detection: the vault's
instant cap (only a capped total leaves instantly per hour; the rest
queues), and the RSC's cross-chain pace freeze. A still slower drain
(e.g. one sub-threshold withdrawal per day to a known address) is not
detected by any method here and is disclosed as a residual risk.

## What a wrong verdict costs

- False MEDIUM: exactly one withdrawal is vetoed. The owner re-requests
  it; nothing else stops. Cost: one transaction and a delay.
- False HIGH: all three vaults freeze. Recovery needs the offline
  recovery key: 3 unfreeze transactions (plus a verdictWriter rotation
  if the writer key is suspected). Funds are never at risk, but the
  vaults are unavailable until the owner acts. At the daily-small
  profile's pace (~2 withdrawals/day), the held-out HIGH counts above
  translate directly into expected freeze frequency: model-v1's 6 HIGH
  in 236 normal withdrawals is roughly one full freeze every 20 days.

## Adoption decisions (criteria fixed before running)

- v2 over v1: (i) HIGH on set A: v1 6 of 236 vs v2 0 of 236 -> PASS; (ii) no attack flagged later -> PASS; (iii) 3x-known probe: MEDIUM -> PASS. **ADOPT v2 over v1**
- v3 over v2: (i) HIGH on set A: v3 0 of 236 vs v2 0 of 236 -> PASS; (ii) slow drain flagged by v3 at step 2 of 20 -> PASS; (iii) no attack flagged later -> FAIL; (iv) 3x-known probe: LOW -> PASS.

**Deployment decision: model-v3**, with one explicit override. Criterion
(iii) FAILS as written: in the concentration scenario v3 flags one step
later than v2 (#2 instead of #1). We adopt v3 anyway, and say so here
rather than editing the criterion, because: (1) the blind spot v3 closes
is total — under v2 the slow drain is NEVER flagged in 20 steps — while
the concentration delay costs at most one extra queued withdrawal
(0.015 ETH, 3% of the demo balance), and queued withdrawals sit in the
timelock where the #2 verdict still vetoes the rest; (2) v3 is the only
model with zero false HIGHs on every held-out set, including
monthly-large (v2: 2 of 152); (3) on the rare-legit probes v3 passes
both as LOW where v2 says MEDIUM. The failed criterion stays on the
record above.

All model files stay in the repo (model/model_v1.json, model_v2.json,
model_v3.json); model/model.json is a copy of the adopted one.
