# Evaluation — rule-v1 vs model (synthetic data)

All traffic is synthetic, generated by model/gen_data.py at the
daily-small scale (the deployed profile). Numbers are reported
as computed, including where the model loses.

## Held-out normal traffic (n=236)

| method | LOW | MEDIUM | HIGH | flagged (>=MEDIUM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rule-v1 | 234 | 0 | 2 | 0.8% |
| model | 227 | 3 | 6 | 3.8% |

## Attacks (withdrawal # at first flag)

| scenario | rule >=MED | model >=MED | rule HIGH | model HIGH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full drain: 0.475 ETH to fresh addr | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| barrage: 10 x 0.004 ETH to fresh addr, 5 min apart | 1 | 1 | - | 1 |
| concentration: 5 x 0.015 ETH to one fresh addr, 1 h apart | 1 | 1 | 1 | - |

## Rare-but-legit (LOW = passed through)

| scenario | rule-v1 | model |
|---|---|---|
| 3x median amount to a known address | LOW | HIGH |
| normal amount after a 30-day pause | LOW | LOW |

Notes: a MEDIUM verdict vetoes only that one withdrawal and the
owner can unfreeze/override; two MEDIUMs within an hour escalate
to a full freeze by design. A HIGH on a rare-legit withdrawal is
therefore costly but recoverable — and it is reported here as-is.
